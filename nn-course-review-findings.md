You asked whether some exercises made you implement things you did not need, and whether you were
fighting Python instead of learning neural networks. Three reviewers went after that specifically,

Five of the nine are clean. The four flagged `partly` are flagged for **different reasons**, and
only one of them is about notation:

- **`prepare` (m10) is the only high-plumbing exercise in the course**, and it is the one whose
  notation complaint is fully verified: `np.where` is required for a branch the penguin data never
  reaches and is taught nowhere; and `split`'s cut-from-the-end is capitalised in the skeleton with
  no reason attached, enforced by no test, yet it silently decides whether your panel prints the
  42.6 percent and the 0-of-18 the prose beside it quotes. That is the shape you described exactly:
  implementing a specification rather than an idea, where the specification is never taught as one.
- **`l2` (m7) is the pure 'did I need this?' case, and it is a framing failure, not a code one.**
  The prompt opens by naming the exercise's size instead of its purchase ('The smallest exercise in
  the course'), dictates four lines you were just shown, and gives as its two reasons that a panel
  downstream needs them. The concept is genuinely there (decay as one factor, biases exempt,
  lambda=0 must recover Module 3 exactly); the prompt just never says so.
- **`cross-entropy` (m7)**: the prompt's opening reason for writing the cost is 'so the panels below
  have something to chart', read by someone handed `delta = a - y` two screens earlier.
- **`sgd` (m3)**: the prescribed shuffle protocol is justified to the learner by the test harness
  ('because the tests replay your run and compare to six decimal places') rather than by the idea
  that makes it necessary.

**On `train` (m9) rewriting `sgd`'s loop, the suspicion I raised from the beat map: refuted.** The
reviewers checked and the module pre-empts it correctly at module9.tsx:32-36 (sgd 'has never drawn

signposted in the module page and in hint 2, and signposted *wrongly* in only one place: the
prompt's opening paragraph, one clause of which is also simply false (it claims the panels cut the

**What the defense pass killed.** Of 26 recommendations: 14 stand, 7 weakened, 5 refuted. Every
proposal that would have changed an exercise's scope or dependencies lost: flipping `quadratic_cost`
to the learner came out weakened on three unpriced costs, and reducing `split` to 'just the idea'
was refuted four ways including self-contradiction. The surviving changes are all prompt wording,
notation rows, and test assertions.

**Bottom line: no exercise should be cut, moved, or rescoped.** Your feeling traces to four
prompts that justify work by pointing at the test harness or a downstream panel instead of at the
idea, plus one exercise (`prepare`) with genuine untaught notation.

## Part 2. The four confirmed high-severity findings

### H1. The gloss's pull directions are inverted: it says the stay corners pull the bias up and the go corners pull it down, but by Module 3's own sign rule the positive stay-corner slopes push the bias down and the negative go-corner slopes push it up.

`src/modules/NN/module5.tsx` — line 66 (the gloss of the four-corner average Eq in m5-cost)

**Evidence.** The gloss reads "The two stay corners pull the bias up, the two go corners pull it down harder, and the average of the four is the -0.044 Module 3 measured". Verified numerically: the stay corners' slopes are +0.0460 and the go corners' are -0.1337, and Module 3 (module3.tsx line 78) taught "Its minus sign says the cost falls when this knob goes up", so a negative slope means the knob should rise. The go corners (negative) are what pull the bias up; the average -0.04385 moves the bias up under descent. The learner is doing the module's one worked numeric check while holding Module 3's sign rule, and the gloss tells them the opposite; the following paragraph's "(The opposite pulls above...)" repeats the framing.

**Direction of fix.** Either flip the directions (stay corners, positive slopes, pull the bias down; go corners pull it up harder) or retarget the verb at the average rather than the bias (the stay corners pull the average up toward positive, the go corners drag it below zero), and reconcile the "opposite pulls" parenthesis two sentences later.

### H2. The 16-cell eta-by-setup grid has no committed bench and no panel: 14 of its 16 cells cannot be regenerated by anything in the repository, and the prose does load-bearing arithmetic on them.

`src/modules/NN/module7.tsx` — lines 903-957 (the "Here is the trying" table and the best-of-each-row sentence)

**Evidence.** tools/bench_depth.py's SECTIONS dict (lines 434-444) holds exactly nine sections: mistakes, depth, first-epoch, eta, ratios, relu, dead, module7, capstone. None varies the cost function or the starting draw. Bench.run() (bench_depth.py lines 213-238) always builds the network with self.init.init_network (the divided start) and always reaches make_engine's sigmoid path, which calls lib.batch_gradient(..., ce.cross_entropy_delta): it cannot produce a quadratic-cost row, and its uniform weight_scale cannot produce the undivided start (that needs sqrt(784) on layer 0 and sqrt(30) on layer 1). Only two of the sixteen cells are covered anywhere: quadratic/undivided/eta 3.0 = 89.2% via bench_mistakes (line 356: "108 of the thousand are read wrong"), and cross-entropy/divided/eta 0.5 = 92.1% via bench_module7. The other fourteen (70.8, 79.4, 89.9, 87.8, 89.3, 87.6, 80.9, 90.0, 90.3, 91.0, 91.3, 92.3, 87.7, 69.4) exist only as literals in the TSX. No panel produces them either: CostSwapPanel runs three fixed configs at 8 epochs, InitStartPanel two fixed configs at 15, DepthTrainPanel a fixed 2-by-2, FullTrainPanel cross-entropy + divided + lambda 1. The prose then argues from them twice: "the best of each row runs 89.9, 89.3, 91.3, 92.3, so the two changes together are worth about two and a half points" (line 956) and "two of the four rows span nineteen points or more" (line 958). Every cell is also a single epoch-15 score, the statistic module8.tsx:44-46 says "wobbles by a point or two".

**Direction of fix.** Add a bench_grid section to tools/bench_depth.py that trains the four setups at the four step sizes and prints the table, and give it a claim line quoting all sixteen cells plus the four best-of-row values. That requires teaching Bench.run() an undivided start (a per-layer scale list, not one scalar) and a quadratic-blame engine, which is the same seam bench_mistakes already opens by calling batch_gradient with no delta argument.

### H3. The regularization section's last-twenty-epoch averages have no committed bench, and its shuffle-wobble numbers cannot be produced by the panel either, yet they carry the section's conclusion and are re-quoted in Module 9.

`src/modules/NN/module7.tsx` — lines 862-876 (the weight-decay reading, "85.7 percent averaged over the last twenty epochs against 86.4")

**Evidence.** No section of tools/bench_depth.py trains on the 1,000-image slice or with a non-zero lambda for 80 epochs (SECTIONS, lines 434-444; bench_capstone uses lambda 1.0 but 5,000 images and 15 epochs). tools/check_panels.py:52 only smoke-runs RegularizePanel with two mixed configs. RegularizePanel itself reports only epoch-80 values: its table header is "after {EPOCHS} epochs" and it renders s.test_accuracy / s.test_cost / s.weight_size from the run's final state (RegularizePanel.tsx lines 296-318), so 85.7 and 86.4 are averages of twenty chart points that nothing in the repo computes. Worse, line 870-871 says "two other shuffles of the same unregularized run give 86.0 and 85.4", and the panel's snippet hardcodes the shuffle: `_rng = np.random.default_rng(2)` (RegularizePanel.tsx line 62), with no control over it. So those two numbers fail both halves of the rule: no bench produces them, and the reader cannot produce them either. They are load-bearing twice: they support "That gap is smaller than the run-to-run wobble in any case" (line 869), which is what lets the section conclude decay costs nothing from the divided start; and module9.tsx:130-132 spends them again ("three shuffles of one unchanged setting landed between 85.4 and 86.4 percent there") to explain away the 90.4-vs-92.1 gap.

**Direction of fix.** Add a bench_regularize section that reproduces RegularizePanel's six runs and prints, per run, the last-twenty-epoch average alongside the epoch-80 value, plus the two extra shuffle seeds behind 86.0 and 85.4 (which currently exist nowhere). If the extra shuffles are not worth benching, cut that sentence and lean on the 84.7/86.3 epoch-80 pair the panel does print.

### H4. The BP1 seam edit, the course's only edit-your-own-working-code moment, is tested for existence but never for correctness: a wrong substitution branch passes all seven cross-entropy tests, passes every upstream suite, and first surfaces two modules later in train's tests with messages that blame the wrong section.

`src/exercises/cross-entropy/tests.py` — test_cost_and_delta_agree (line 122-178) and test_backprop_takes_the_blame_argument (line 181-216)

**Evidence.** test_cost_and_delta_agree does its gradient check with `from course import backprop, numerical_gradient` (line 124), i.e. the course's backprop, so the learner's else-branch is never exercised by it. test_backprop_takes_the_blame_argument only calls backprop with the argument absent and with None (lines 208-209), which both take the if-branch. Verified: patching the seam's else-branch to `delta = output_delta(activations[-1], y, zs[-1]) * sigmoid_prime(zs[-1])` (the natural hand-edit, since the line being replaced ends in that factor and the module says 'one line changes', module7.tsx:458-459) leaves all 7 cross-entropy tests PASS, all of backprop/feedforward/sigmoid-neuron/smart-init/l2 PASS, and only train fails, with 'The order is prescribed: draw the network from rng first...' and 'after 20 epochs the network reads 67%... the updated parameters are probably not being carried forward'. Neither names the seam. This also falsifies the invariant CLAUDE.md relies on for the panel's upstream diagnosis walk ('a section that breaks its dependants always breaks its own suite too'). Meanwhile CostSwapPanel, InitStartPanel and RegularizePanel exec the raw file, so they would chart a cross-entropy run that still slows down, contradicting module7.tsx:526-532 ('62.6 percent against the cross-entropy run's 86.5').

**Direction of fix.** Add one assertion to test_cost_and_delta_agree (or a new test) that runs the LEARNER's backprop, `submission.backprop(weights, biases, x, y, cross_entropy_delta)`, against numerical_gradient of the learner's cross_entropy_cost. It costs one extra call and turns a two-module-late mystery into a message at the seam.
